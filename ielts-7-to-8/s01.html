Decision 2

Your if-then plan

2 min

Stated implementation intentions roughly double follow-through compared with an intention to "study more". Write yours as a sentence with a real trigger — an existing habit, not a time you hope to be free.

Decision 3

Predict your baseline

2 min

Before you do anything, guess your raw score out of 40 for today's listening and reading. You are almost certainly going to be wrong, and that is the point: calibration is a trainable skill, and over-confidence is what produces the unchecked wrong answer on test day.

25–45 min · Reading

True / False / Not Given — the three-way test

This week's text: the electricity that artificial intelligence is starting to consume, and the disagreement about how much it will consume next. Thirteen questions, twenty minutes.

R1

Gist — sixty seconds, questions hidden

2 min

Skim “The machines that got thirsty”, the reading passage below. The button takes you straight to it and hides the questions for sixty seconds. Then return here and write one sentence: what is this text arguing?

Why: a gist gives every later question a frame to sit in. Reading questions first is faster per question and slower per passage.

R2

Triage — classify before you answer

2 min

Two question types today. Before answering any of them, notice which is which and where each answer lives. TFNG answers follow the order of the passage; they live in one line. Completion answers also follow the passage order and live in one phrase. Neither requires you to have understood the whole text.

The rule that decides every TFNG item:
TRUE — you can quote a line that says this.
FALSE — you can quote a line that contradicts this.
NOT GIVEN — you searched where it would have to be, and there is no such line.
The trap is not FALSE versus TRUE. It is FALSE versus NOT GIVEN, and the test is always the same: can I point at the contradiction? If you cannot point at it, it is NOT GIVEN.

R3 + R4

Hunt — then prove it

12 min

Twenty minutes on the clock, sixty seconds maximum per item. If an item takes longer, flag it and move — that reflex is worth more marks than any amount of vocabulary. Every answer needs a quote. The key will not unlock until each item has one, because an answer you cannot prove is an answer you got by luck.

45–67 min · Speaking

Part 1 — the two-sentence floor

The most common band-7 pattern in Part 1 is the one-sentence answer, followed by silence, followed by the examiner rescuing you. The fix is a floor and a ceiling: never one sentence, never a speech.

S1

Model — hear it, then see why

3 min

One Part 1 question, answered at band 8. Listen first, then read the annotation.

Model answer · "Do you use much technology at home?"
A twenty-six second answer. Count the moves.

"Far more than I'd like, honestly. ← position, in four words, with a personal angle
There's a smart speaker in almost every room, which my flatmate installed and I've quietly grown dependent on. ← one concrete specific + a relative clause doing real work
What surprised me was how quickly it stopped feeling like technology at all — it just became how the lights go on. ← the extension: a reflection that turns an answer into a conversation"

Three moves, twenty-six seconds. Not a speech. Notice that nothing in it is impressive vocabulary — quietly grown dependent on and it just became how the lights go on are ordinary words in precise arrangements. That is what band 8 lexis actually sounds like.

S2

Mine — five chunks, rewritten as yours

3 min

Take each frame and fill it with your content. A chunk you have never personalised will not arrive when you need it.

S3

Plan — sixty seconds, then say the first line

2 min

You get eight questions. Before recording, say your opening line for question 1 out loud, once. Speaking the first sentence aloud before the take removes most of the first-answer stumble.

Today's rule: every answer is answer + reason or answer + example. Target 20–30 seconds. Under 10 seconds is a band-7 answer no matter how correct it is; over 45 seconds in Part 1 is a different problem with the same cause.

S4 → S6

The two-take loop

14 min

Record. Audit. Change exactly one thing. Record again. Not three things — one. A learner who tries to fix everything fixes nothing, hears no improvement, and stops recording by Session 4. One change is audible. That is the whole design.

    67–87 min · Writing

    Task 2 — the thesis sentence, alone

    Twenty minutes, one element, no essay. You will write three thesis sentences today and nothing else. This is deliberate: an essay produces feedback on six things at once, and feedback on six things changes nothing.

    W1

    See it — the same prompt, two bands

    3 min
    Prompt: Some people believe that the rapid growth of energy-hungry technologies should be restricted by governments. Others argue that innovation should not be limited in this way. Discuss both views and give your own opinion.
    Band 7 thesis
    "This essay will discuss both views about restricting energy-hungry technologies and will explain why I believe that some regulation is necessary."
    Correct, clear, and on topic — and it does almost nothing. It announces a topic and a direction. "Some regulation is necessary" commits to no particular regulation, in no particular circumstance, for no particular reason.
    Band 8 thesis
    "While restricting emerging technologies risks stalling the very innovation that makes them efficient, governments have a legitimate role where a single industry's growth begins to distort a shared resource — and electricity is precisely such a resource."
    It states a position, and it states its scope: not "regulation is necessary" but regulation where growth distorts a shared resource. The essay that follows now has a job it can be judged against.
    W2

    Name it — one rule, your words

    2 min

    Write the difference as a single sentence. Do not scroll back up. Articulating the rule yourself is worth several times more than reading it, and it is the step everyone skips.

    A band-7 thesis announces the topic. A band-8 thesis states a position and its scope. Scope is the word that does the work: the conditions under which your position holds. It is also what gives you two body paragraphs that are genuinely different from each other rather than the same claim twice.

    W3

    Do it — three theses, no essays

    7 min

    Three prompts, three thesis sentences. Roughly two minutes each. Do not write an introduction, do not write a body paragraph, do not plan the essay.

    W4

    Score it — four checks, this element only

    4 min

    Score each of your three sentences. Nothing about grammar, nothing about vocabulary, nothing about the rest of the essay. Only whether it is a thesis.

    W5

    Rewrite — the weakest one, once

    4 min

    Take whichever sentence scored lowest and rewrite it. Once. Then stop — you are done writing for today, and that is by design, not by omission.
